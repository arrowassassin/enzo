## 2. Ideas

Scores: Market / Timing / Lab-immunity / Whitespace / Moat / Capital-efficiency / Demand-evidence.

### Idea 1 — Private-credit portfolio-monitoring agent + borrower-data network
**Pitch:** The system of record that ingests borrower compliance packages (PDF financials, covenant certificates, emails), re-computes covenants, flags breaches, and becomes the shared data pipe between lender and borrower.
**Why now:** AUM >$2T with cov-lite at 21% means more loans with less protection and thin monitoring teams; the 38-point underwriting-vs-monitoring gap is documented. Ellis's seed validates investor appetite.
**Competitors:** Ellis (seed), Lumonic/PitchBook, legacy Allvue/Black Mountain. Low-moderate.
**Lab immunity:** Labs have no borrower relationship, no loan-agreement corpus, no fund-admin integrations.
**Scores:** 7 / 8 / 7 / 6 / 8 / 6 / 6.

### Idea 2 — Specialty commercial underwriting workbench for MGAs
**Pitch:** Submission-to-quote agent for E&S/specialty lines: parses broker submissions, loss runs, SOVs; applies carrier appetite; drafts bind-ready quotes.
**Why now:** Underwriting automation drew only 4 deals vs 12 broker and 11 claims.
**Competitors:** Federato, Sixfold, legacy Guidewire. Moderate.
**Scores:** 7 / 7 / 7 / 6 / 7 / 5 / 5.

### Idea 3 — "Integration factory" for AI roll-up platforms (picks-and-shovels)
**Pitch:** A services+software company that migrates and unifies acquired firms' legacy stacks and deploys the AI layer for roll-up platforms — sold to the roll-ups, not to end firms.
**Why now:** $1.5B Creation fund plus Current's $500M acquisition budget imply hundreds of bolt-on integrations per year; each is a bespoke migration nobody is productising.
**Scores:** 5 / 8 / 9 / 8 / 6 / 8 / 4.

### Idea 4 — AI-native accounting/tax firm roll-up outside Current's hunting ground
**Pitch:** Own, don't sell to, sub-$5M-revenue firms in a niche Current/GC ignore (industry-specific practices, or non-US English-speaking markets).
**Scores:** 8 / 7 / 10 / 4 / 7 / 3 / 7.

**Verdict:** The sector's obvious layers — legal research, customer support, freight voice, clinical scribing — are decided at $1–16B valuations and are exactly where lab enterprise suites will keep pushing. The durable openings are where a system of record does not yet exist and the work is document- and relationship-bound: private-credit monitoring, specialty underwriting, and the plumbing behind the roll-up wave itself. Given the search limits, the top three should be re-validated with 10–15 practitioner interviews before any commitment.
